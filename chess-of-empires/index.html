Actions per turn

Against the computer you play White and move first.

The Age of Empires look draws units and buildings as pictures with hit points as a bar of segments, and words the hints that way. The 3D view keeps chess pieces.

Map

Rock cannot be entered, stood on or built on. Every map is mirrored across the a8–h1 diagonal, so both sides have exactly the same paths.

Rules in brief

Goal. Hit the enemy Town Center when it stands bare, or let your opponent start a turn with no villagers and no way to train one.

Setup. Each side starts with its Town Center and two villagers two squares away along the edges (White a3 and c1, Black h6 and f8): each is one move from the fields or the forest. White moves first, with a single action on its first turn. Maps: open, rocks 2×2, cross, ridge, bar, boulders, bastions, diagonal, gorge, walled homes, rocky fields, labyrinth or rocky rim — chosen in the New game menu. Nobody may enter, stand or build on rock.

Your turn

  • Two actions per turn, for anything: gathering, building, training, advancing, moving or fighting.
  • Each piece acts once. At most two pieces attack one target, each paying one action.
  • A unit may move before attacking; the attack uses one step of its speed. Archers and trebuchets shoot from where they stand.

Checkers: age and hit points

  • Bare is Dark, one checker is Feudal, two is Castle. Imperial swaps one for gold underneath.
  • A hit sends the top checker back to its owner’s reserve (gold back to the supply). A bare piece dies to the next hit.
  • Only the Town Center keeps a lost checker beside it: an adjacent villager slides it back for 1 wood, and a damaged Town Center cannot advance. Other pieces buy a lost checker again at its normal price.

Resources

  • Food buys people and horses: every chess-piece unit and its checkers. Wood buys the empire: buildings, their checkers, the ages and machines (the trebuchet). Gold is the gold checker.
  • Everything gathers on 4+. Your home (3×3: White a1–c3, Black f6–h8) gives nothing. A villager in the food field (a5–d8, plus a4–c4 and e6–e8) gathers food; in the wood field (e1–h4, plus d1–d3 and f5–h5) wood. Food and wood hold 0–6 each.
  • One mill and one lumber camp, in the fields and the forest only: for one action every villager next to it, standing in the same field or forest, rolls. The Town Center is not a mill.
  • No dice: gathering and attacks always succeed. Nothing is rolled.
  • Gold: from the Castle Age a villager standing on one of the four centre squares (d4, e4, d5, e5) takes one of the opponent’s reserve checkers as gold on 4+. Or trade: with a villager on a8 (food post) or h1 (wood post), buy gold for one action: pay 6 of that resource, or 6 in any mix if you hold both posts (no dice) and take one of the opponent’s reserve checkers as gold. Anyone may stand on a corner. If their reserve is empty, the gold checker comes out of their food or wood instead, their choice. Hold at most 2 gold at any time; spend as much as you like. Spent gold goes back to its owner’s reserve.

Ages

  • One action at the Town Center.
  • Feudal: 3 wood and a standing mill or lumber camp. Castle: 5 wood and a standing barracks. Imperial: 4 wood + 1 gold and a standing castle.

Units

  • Villager: a pawn bought at the Town Center for 1 food, attack 1. A villager next to the barracks becomes a unit for one action.
  • The chess piece names the unit: pawn infantry, bishop archer (moves 2 or shoots exactly 2), knight cavalry (moves 3 through free squares, no jumping), steel coin trebuchet (once your castle stands, one at a time): it arrives at Castle height, the coin on two checkers, so 5 wood (coin 3 + two checkers); 2 hit points, attack 1, ignoring building defence. No heavy step: in the Imperial Age a gold checker makes it elite, +1 attack.
  • The checkers give the level: one checker is the base unit; a second checker (Castle Age) makes it heavy, +1 hit point; a gold checker (Imperial Age) makes it elite, +1 attack, and your own checker goes back.
  • One price per checker: 1 food on a unit, 1 wood on a building or machine, 1 gold for a gold checker. One price per piece: pawn 1 food, bishop 1 food, knight 2 food, coin 3 wood, rook 3 wood, queen 4 wood. So an archer is bishop 1 food + checker 1 food; cavalry 3 food.
  • At most two archers, two cavalry and one trebuchet.

Buildings

  • Barracks: home only (White a1–c3, Black f6–h8), built two checkers high: 2 wood + 2 checkers. Mill and lumber camp: 1 wood + 1 checker, from the Dark Age; one of each at most.
  • A crown is a chess piece set on a building. Watch tower: from the Feudal Age a rook on a mill or camp, 3 wood; shoots exactly 2, one action. It climbs like a unit: a second checker (1 wood) from the Castle Age, +1 hit point; a gold checker in the Imperial Age, +1 attack. Castle: from the Castle Age the queen on the barracks, 4 wood; shoots 1–2, one action.
  • A crowned building shoots while the piece stands; hits take its checkers first. Shots deal 1, or 2 with gold. The Town Center shoots 1–2 away, like the castle, from the start, for one action.
  • The trebuchet is a coin, not a rook: its checkers are its hit points. Moves 1 in any direction, shoots exactly 3 away, straight or diagonal only. It ignores building defence.

Combat

  • Attack 1 per piece, +1 with gold. Damage = total attack − defence.
  • Buildings have defence 1, so a villager hurts one only with a second attacker. Everyone may attack from the very start, villagers included; the Town Center too. No striking back.
