#VIII|Chapter VIII|Cooperation, MDCG, expert panels and registers|Art. 101–108
What must Member States do regarding competent authorities (Art. 101)?|Designate the competent authority or authorities for implementing the MDR, give them the necessary powers, resources, equipment and knowledge, and communicate their names and contact details to the Commission, which publishes a list.|Art. 101
What does Art. 102 require on cooperation?|Competent authorities cooperate with each other and with the Commission, which organises the exchange of information needed for uniform application. Member States take part in international initiatives (e.g. IMDRF) on cooperation between regulators.|Art. 102
What is the composition of the Medical Device Coordination Group (MDCG)?|Each Member State appoints one member and one alternate with expertise in medical devices, and one member and one alternate with expertise in IVDs (one person may cover both). Members serve three-year renewable terms. A Commission representative chairs without voting. The MDCG may set up sub-groups.|Art. 103
Name four tasks of the MDCG.|Contribute to assessing conformity assessment bodies and notified bodies; contribute to guidance for harmonised implementation (MDCG guidance documents), e.g. on notified bodies, GSPR, clinical evaluation and investigation, and vigilance; monitor technical progress; assist authorities in coordinating clinical investigation and vigilance; advise the Commission on implementation; contribute to standards and common specifications.|Art. 105
What do expert panels do under the MDR?|Designated by the Commission in relevant medical fields, they give scientific opinions in the clinical evaluation consultation procedure (Art. 54), advise manufacturers on clinical development strategy (Art. 61(2)), and advise on common specifications and guidance. Since 2022 the EMA provides their secretariat (Regulation (EU) 2022/123).|Art. 106
What conflict-of-interest rules apply to MDCG members and experts?|They must have no financial or other interests in the medical device industry that could affect their impartiality, must act in the public interest and independently, and must declare their direct and indirect interests, keep the declaration up to date, and have it published on the Commission website.|Art. 107
What does Art. 108 say about device registers?|The Commission and Member States encourage registers and databanks for specific types of devices, with common principles for comparable data, to support independent evaluation of long-term safety and performance and the traceability of implantable devices.|Art. 108
What support does the Commission give the MDCG (Art. 104)?|It supports cooperation between national competent authorities, organises exchanges of experience, and provides technical, scientific and logistic support to the MDCG and its sub-groups.|Art. 104
