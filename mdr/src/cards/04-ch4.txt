#IV|Chapter IV|Notified bodies|Art. 35–50
What is the role of the authority responsible for notified bodies?|Each Member State that designates notified bodies appoints an authority responsible for setting up and carrying out the procedures to assess, designate and notify conformity assessment bodies and to monitor notified bodies, including their subcontractors and subsidiaries. It must be objective, impartial and free of conflicts of interest with the bodies it assesses.|Art. 35
Where are the requirements that notified bodies must meet laid down?|In Art. 36 and Annex VII: organisational and general requirements (legal status, independence and impartiality, confidentiality, liability insurance, financial resources, participation in coordination), quality management, resources (qualified personnel, subcontractors) and process requirements.|Art. 36
Describe the joint assessment in the designation of a notified body.|After the national authority reviews the application, the Commission (with the MDCG) appoints a joint assessment team, normally of three experts from the list of qualified experts, with at least one Commission representative coordinating. The team assesses the applicant and the national report; the MDCG gives a recommendation before designation and notification.|Art. 38–40
How is a notified body identified, and where are they listed?|The Commission assigns each notified body an identification number (one number even if notified under several Union acts) and publishes the list of notified bodies with their numbers and scopes in the NANDO database.|Art. 43
How often are notified bodies monitored and fully re-assessed?|The national authority monitors each notified body at least once a year (incl. on-site audits). A complete re-assessment by the national authority and a joint assessment team takes place three years after notification and every fourth year thereafter.|Art. 44(4), (10)
What does the review under Art. 45 involve?|The national authority reviews an appropriate number of the notified body's assessments of manufacturers' technical documentation, in particular clinical evaluation documentation, to verify the notified body's conclusions. The Commission may also review such assessments.|Art. 45
What happens to certificates when a notified body's designation is suspended or withdrawn?|The national authority restricts, suspends or withdraws the designation and informs the Commission and Member States. Unduly issued certificates are suspended or withdrawn. Other certificates may remain valid temporarily under conditions, e.g. after a restriction or withdrawal for nine months (extendable in steps of three months to at most twelve) if the competent authority confirms there is no safety issue and another notified body takes over responsibility.|Art. 46
What is the coordination group of notified bodies?|The Commission ensures coordination and cooperation between notified bodies through a coordination group of notified bodies (NBCG-Med), in which notified bodies participate.|Art. 49
What must notified bodies publish about their fees?|Lists of their standard fees for the conformity assessment activities they carry out.|Art. 50
Name two independence rules for notified bodies (Annex VII).|A notified body is a third-party body independent of the manufacturer and of other economic operators with an interest in the device. It, its top management and assessment staff must not design, manufacture, supply, install, buy, own or maintain the devices they assess, nor be the authorised representative of such parties, and must not provide consultancy to manufacturers on the devices or processes under assessment.|Annex VII 1.2
