#AVIII|Annex VIII|Classification rules|Annex VIII
How many classification rules are there and how are they grouped?|22 rules: non-invasive devices (Rules 1–4), invasive devices (Rules 5–8), active devices (Rules 9–13) and special rules (Rules 14–22).|Annex VIII Ch. III
Define transient, short-term and long-term use.|Transient: normally intended for continuous use for less than 60 minutes. Short-term: between 60 minutes and 30 days. Long-term: more than 30 days.|Annex VIII 1.1–1.3
Which rule applies if several classification rules apply to one device?|The strictest rule and sub-rule, giving the higher class.|Annex VIII 3.5
How are accessories and software classified?|Accessories are classified in their own right, separately from the device they are used with. Software that drives or influences the use of a device has the same class as that device; standalone software is classified in its own right.|Annex VIII 3.2–3.3
State Rule 1.|All non-invasive devices are class I, unless one of the other rules applies.|Annex VIII Rule 1
State Rule 2.|Non-invasive devices for channelling or storing blood, body liquids, cells or tissues, liquids or gases for eventual infusion, administration or introduction into the body are class IIa if they may be connected to a class IIa, IIb or III active device, or if used for channelling or storing blood or other body liquids or storing organs, parts of organs or body cells and tissues; blood bags are class IIb. Otherwise class I.|Annex VIII Rule 2
State Rule 3.|Non-invasive devices modifying the biological or chemical composition of human tissues or cells, blood, other body liquids or other liquids intended for implantation or administration into the body are class IIb, or class IIa if the treatment is filtration, centrifugation or exchange of gas or heat. Substances used in vitro in direct contact with human cells, tissues or organs, or with embryos, before implantation or administration are class III.|Annex VIII Rule 3
State Rule 4.|Non-invasive devices contacting injured skin or mucous membrane: class I as a mechanical barrier, for compression or for absorption of exudates; class IIb if mainly for wounds that breach the dermis or mucous membrane and can only heal by secondary intent; class IIa if mainly to manage the micro-environment of injured skin or mucous membrane; class IIa in all other cases.|Annex VIII Rule 4
State Rule 5.|Invasive devices via body orifices (not surgically invasive), not connected to an active device or connected to a class I active device: transient use class I; short-term class IIa (class I in the oral cavity up to the pharynx, ear canal up to the eardrum, or nasal cavity); long-term class IIb (class IIa in those cavities if not absorbable by the mucous membrane). If connected to a class IIa, IIb or III active device: class IIa.|Annex VIII Rule 5
State Rule 6.|Surgically invasive devices for transient use are class IIa, unless: reusable surgical instruments (class I); specifically to control, diagnose, monitor or correct a defect of the heart or central circulatory system by direct contact, or in direct contact with the heart, central circulatory system or central nervous system (class III); supply ionising radiation energy (class IIb); have a biological effect or are wholly or mainly absorbed (class IIb); administer medicinal products in a potentially hazardous way (class IIb).|Annex VIII Rule 6
State Rule 7.|Surgically invasive devices for short-term use are class IIa, unless: for the heart or central circulatory system by direct contact, or in direct contact with the heart, central circulatory system or central nervous system (class III); supply ionising radiation (class IIb); have a biological effect or are wholly or mainly absorbed (class III); undergo chemical change in the body, except in the teeth, or administer medicines (class IIb).|Annex VIII Rule 7
State Rule 8.|Implantable and long-term surgically invasive devices are class IIb, unless: placed in the teeth (class IIa); in direct contact with the heart, central circulatory system or central nervous system (class III); biological effect or wholly or mainly absorbed (class III); chemical change in the body (except teeth) or administering medicines (class III); active implantable devices or their accessories, breast implants, surgical meshes, total or partial joint replacements, spinal disc replacements or implants contacting the spinal column (class III; ancillary screws, wedges, plates and instruments excepted).|Annex VIII Rule 8
State Rule 9.|Active therapeutic devices administering or exchanging energy are class IIa, or class IIb if they do so in a potentially hazardous way (nature, density and site of the energy). Devices controlling, monitoring or directly influencing class IIb active therapeutic devices, or emitting ionising radiation for therapy (and their controllers), are class IIb. Devices controlling, monitoring or directly influencing active implantable devices are class III.|Annex VIII Rule 9
State Rule 10.|Active diagnostic and monitoring devices are class IIa if they supply energy absorbed by the body (class I if only illuminating the body in the visible spectrum), image in vivo distribution of radiopharmaceuticals, or allow direct diagnosis or monitoring of vital physiological processes. Class IIb if monitoring vital parameters whose variations could result in immediate danger, or for diagnosis when the patient is in immediate danger. Devices emitting ionising radiation for diagnostic or interventional radiology are class IIb.|Annex VIII Rule 10
State Rule 11 (software).|Software providing information used to take decisions for diagnosis or therapy is class IIa, unless the decisions may cause death or irreversible deterioration of health (class III), or serious deterioration of health or a surgical intervention (class IIb). Software monitoring physiological processes is class IIa, or class IIb if monitoring vital parameters whose variations could result in immediate danger. All other software is class I.|Annex VIII Rule 11
State Rule 12.|Active devices intended to administer and/or remove medicinal products, body liquids or other substances to or from the body are class IIa, or class IIb if done in a potentially hazardous way (nature of the substances, part of the body, mode of application).|Annex VIII Rule 12
State Rule 13.|All other active devices are class I.|Annex VIII Rule 13
State Rule 14.|Devices incorporating, as an integral part, a substance that used separately would be a medicinal product (incl. one derived from human blood or plasma), with an action ancillary to that of the device, are class III.|Annex VIII Rule 14
State Rule 15.|Devices used for contraception or to prevent the transmission of sexually transmitted diseases are class IIb, or class III if implantable or long-term invasive.|Annex VIII Rule 15
State Rule 16.|Devices specifically for disinfecting, cleaning, rinsing or hydrating contact lenses are class IIb. Devices specifically for disinfecting or sterilising medical devices are class IIa, or class IIb if they are disinfecting solutions or washer-disinfectors for disinfecting invasive devices as the end point of processing. Not for devices cleaning other devices (than contact lenses) by physical action only.|Annex VIII Rule 16
State Rule 17.|Devices specifically intended for recording diagnostic images generated by X-ray radiation are class IIa.|Annex VIII Rule 17
State Rule 18.|Devices manufactured utilising non-viable (or rendered non-viable) tissues or cells of human or animal origin, or their derivatives, are class III, unless made from animal tissues or derivatives and intended to contact intact skin only.|Annex VIII Rule 18
State Rule 19.|Devices incorporating or consisting of nanomaterial are class III if the potential for internal exposure is high or medium, class IIb if low, and class IIa if negligible.|Annex VIII Rule 19
State Rule 20.|Invasive devices via body orifices (not surgically invasive) intended to administer medicinal products by inhalation are class IIa, or class IIb if their mode of action has an essential impact on the efficacy and safety of the medicine or they treat life-threatening conditions.|Annex VIII Rule 20
State Rule 21.|Substance-based devices introduced via a body orifice or applied to the skin and absorbed or locally dispersed: class III if they or their metabolites are systemically absorbed to achieve the intended purpose, or act in the stomach or lower GI tract and are systemically absorbed; class IIa if applied to the skin or in the nasal or oral cavity up to the pharynx and acting there; class IIb in all other cases.|Annex VIII Rule 21
State Rule 22.|Active therapeutic devices with an integrated or incorporated diagnostic function that significantly determines patient management by the device, such as closed-loop systems or automated external defibrillators, are class III.|Annex VIII Rule 22
Classify: a short-term urinary catheter, not connected to an active device.|Class IIa: invasive via a body orifice, not surgically invasive, short-term use (Rule 5).|Annex VIII Rule 5
Classify: a tongue depressor.|Class I: invasive via a body orifice for transient use (Rule 5).|Annex VIII Rule 5
Classify: a reusable scalpel.|Class I (Ir): a reusable surgical instrument (Rule 6). A notified body assesses only the reuse aspects.|Annex VIII Rule 6
Classify: a blood bag.|Class IIb (Rule 2).|Annex VIII Rule 2
Classify: a dressing for a deep wound that can only heal by secondary intent.|Class IIb (Rule 4).|Annex VIII Rule 4
Classify: a dental filling.|Class IIa: an implantable device placed in the teeth (Rule 8).|Annex VIII Rule 8
Classify: a breast implant, a hip joint replacement and a heart valve.|All class III (Rule 8): breast implants and total or partial joint replacements are named explicitly; a heart valve is in direct contact with the heart or central circulatory system.|Annex VIII Rule 8
Classify: a condom.|Class IIb: prevention of sexually transmitted disease and contraception (Rule 15).|Annex VIII Rule 15
Classify: a disinfecting solution for contact lenses.|Class IIb (Rule 16).|Annex VIII Rule 16
Classify: an automated external defibrillator (AED).|Class III: an active therapeutic device with an integrated diagnostic function that significantly determines patient management (Rule 22).|Annex VIII Rule 22
Classify: software that supports diagnosis where a wrong decision could cause a serious deterioration of health.|Class IIb (Rule 11); class III if the decision could cause death or irreversible deterioration; class IIa otherwise.|Annex VIII Rule 11
Classify: a digital X-ray detector that records diagnostic images.|Class IIa (Rule 17).|Annex VIII Rule 17
Classify: corrective spectacle lenses.|Class I: non-invasive, no other rule applies (Rule 1).|Annex VIII Rule 1
