#I|Chapter I|Scope and definitions|Art. 1–4
What is the MDR's full legal reference?|Regulation (EU) 2017/745 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 5 April 2017 on medical devices, published in OJ L 117 of 5 May 2017.|Title
Which two directives did the MDR repeal?|Council Directive 90/385/EEC on active implantable medical devices (AIMDD) and Council Directive 93/42/EEC on medical devices (MDD).|Art. 122
Which regulation covers in vitro diagnostic medical devices instead of the MDR?|Regulation (EU) 2017/746, the IVDR. IVDs are excluded from the MDR's scope.|Art. 1(6)(a)
When did the MDR enter into force, and from when does it apply?|It entered into force on 25 May 2017 (20 days after publication). It applies from 26 May 2021. The original date was 26 May 2020; Regulation (EU) 2020/561 postponed it by one year because of COVID-19.|Art. 123
Give the key elements of the definition of a "medical device".|Any instrument, apparatus, appliance, software, implant, reagent, material or other article intended by the manufacturer to be used, alone or in combination, for human beings for a specific medical purpose: diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment or alleviation of disease; diagnosis, monitoring, treatment, alleviation of or compensation for an injury or disability; investigation, replacement or modification of the anatomy or of a physiological or pathological process or state; or providing information by in vitro examination of specimens from the human body. It does not achieve its principal intended action by pharmacological, immunological or metabolic means, but may be assisted in its function by such means.|Art. 2(1)
Which two groups of products are also deemed to be medical devices under Art. 2(1)?|Devices for the control or support of conception, and products specifically intended for the cleaning, disinfection or sterilisation of devices.|Art. 2(1)
Define "accessory for a medical device".|An article which, while not itself a medical device, is intended by its manufacturer to be used together with one or several particular medical devices to specifically enable them to be used in accordance with their intended purpose, or to specifically and directly assist their medical functionality in terms of their intended purpose.|Art. 2(2)
Define "intended purpose".|The use for which a device is intended according to the data supplied by the manufacturer on the label, in the instructions for use or in promotional or sales materials or statements, and as specified by the manufacturer in the clinical evaluation.|Art. 2(12)
Define "manufacturer".|A natural or legal person who manufactures or fully refurbishes a device, or has a device designed, manufactured or fully refurbished, and markets that device under its name or trademark.|Art. 2(30)
Who are the "economic operators" under the MDR?|The manufacturer, the authorised representative, the importer, the distributor, and the person who combines devices into systems or procedure packs or sterilises them (Art. 22(1) and (3)).|Art. 2(35)
Define "importer".|Any natural or legal person established within the Union that places a device from a third country on the Union market.|Art. 2(33)
Define "distributor".|Any natural or legal person in the supply chain, other than the manufacturer or the importer, that makes a device available on the market, up until the point of putting into service.|Art. 2(34)
Define "authorised representative".|Any natural or legal person established within the Union who has received and accepted a written mandate from a manufacturer located outside the Union to act on the manufacturer's behalf in relation to specified tasks regarding the manufacturer's obligations under the MDR.|Art. 2(32)
Distinguish "making available on the market", "placing on the market" and "putting into service".|Making available: any supply of a device (other than an investigational device) for distribution, consumption or use on the Union market in the course of a commercial activity, paid or free. Placing on the market: the first making available of a device on the Union market. Putting into service: the stage at which a device has been made available to the final user as ready for use on the Union market for the first time for its intended purpose.|Art. 2(27)–(29)
Define "custom-made device".|A device specifically made in accordance with a written prescription of a person authorised by national law by virtue of their professional qualifications, giving specific design characteristics under that person's responsibility, and intended for the sole use of a particular patient exclusively to meet their individual conditions and needs. Mass-produced devices that are adapted for a professional user, or mass-produced by industrial processes according to written prescriptions, are not custom-made.|Art. 2(3)
Define "implantable device".|Any device, including those partially or wholly absorbed, intended to be totally introduced into the human body, or to replace an epithelial surface or the surface of the eye, by clinical intervention and intended to remain in place after the procedure. A device partially introduced by clinical intervention and intended to remain in place for at least 30 days is also deemed implantable.|Art. 2(5)
Define "invasive device".|Any device which, in whole or in part, penetrates inside the body, either through a body orifice or through the surface of the body.|Art. 2(6)
Define "active device". Is software an active device?|A device whose operation depends on a source of energy other than that generated by the human body for that purpose or by gravity, and which acts by changing the density of or converting that energy. Devices that only transmit energy, substances or other elements between an active device and the patient without significant change are not active devices. Software is deemed to be an active device.|Art. 2(4)
Define "generic device group".|A set of devices having the same or similar intended purposes or a commonality of technology, allowing them to be classified in a generic manner not reflecting specific characteristics.|Art. 2(7)
Define "single-use device".|A device that is intended to be used on one individual during a single procedure.|Art. 2(8)
Define "falsified device".|Any device with a false presentation of its identity and/or of its source and/or of its CE marking certificates or documents relating to CE marking procedures. It does not cover unintentional non-compliance and is without prejudice to infringements of intellectual property rights.|Art. 2(9)
What is the difference between a "procedure pack" and a "system"?|Procedure pack: a combination of products packaged together and placed on the market for use for a specific medical purpose. System: a combination of products, packaged together or not, intended to be inter-connected or combined to achieve a specific medical purpose.|Art. 2(10)–(11)
Define "nanomaterial" under the MDR.|A natural, incidental or manufactured material containing particles in an unbound state, as an aggregate or as an agglomerate, where for 50% or more of the particles in the number size distribution one or more external dimensions is in the range 1–100 nm. Fullerenes, graphene flakes and single-wall carbon nanotubes with one or more external dimensions below 1 nm are also nanomaterials.|Art. 2(18)
Define "risk" and "benefit-risk determination".|Risk: the combination of the probability of occurrence of harm and the severity of that harm. Benefit-risk determination: the analysis of all assessments of benefit and risk of possible relevance for the use of the device for the intended purpose, when used as intended by the manufacturer.|Art. 2(23)–(24)
Define "fully refurbishing".|The complete rebuilding of a device already placed on the market or put into service, or the making of a new device from used devices, to bring it into conformity with the MDR, combined with the assignment of a new lifetime to the refurbished device.|Art. 2(31)
Define "health institution" and "lay person".|Health institution: an organisation whose primary purpose is the care or treatment of patients or the promotion of public health. Lay person: an individual who does not have formal education in a relevant field of healthcare or medical discipline.|Art. 2(36), (38)
Define "reprocessing".|A process carried out on a used device to allow its safe reuse, including cleaning, disinfection, sterilisation and related procedures, as well as testing and restoring the technical and functional safety of the used device.|Art. 2(39)
Define "clinical evaluation".|A systematic and planned process to continuously generate, collect, analyse and assess the clinical data pertaining to a device in order to verify the safety and performance, including clinical benefits, of the device when used as intended by the manufacturer.|Art. 2(44)
Define "clinical investigation" and "sponsor".|Clinical investigation: any systematic investigation involving one or more human subjects, undertaken to assess the safety or performance of a device. Sponsor: any individual, company, institution or organisation which takes responsibility for the initiation, management and setting up of the financing of the clinical investigation.|Art. 2(45), (49)
From which four sources can "clinical data" come?|(1) Clinical investigations of the device concerned; (2) clinical investigations or other studies reported in scientific literature of a device for which equivalence can be demonstrated; (3) reports in peer-reviewed scientific literature on other clinical experience of the device or an equivalent device; (4) clinically relevant information from post-market surveillance, in particular post-market clinical follow-up.|Art. 2(48)
Define "clinical evidence" and "clinical benefit".|Clinical evidence: clinical data and clinical evaluation results of sufficient amount and quality to allow a qualified assessment of whether the device is safe and achieves the intended clinical benefits when used as intended. Clinical benefit: the positive impact of a device on the health of an individual, expressed as meaningful, measurable, patient-relevant clinical outcomes (including diagnosis), or a positive impact on patient management or public health.|Art. 2(51), (53)
Define "serious adverse event" (clinical investigations).|Any adverse event that led to: death; serious deterioration in the health of the subject (life-threatening illness or injury, permanent impairment of a body structure or function, hospitalisation or prolonged hospitalisation, medical or surgical intervention to prevent these, or chronic disease); or foetal distress, foetal death, or a congenital physical or mental impairment or birth defect.|Art. 2(58)
Define "device deficiency".|Any inadequacy in the identity, quality, durability, reliability, safety or performance of an investigational device, including malfunction, use errors or inadequacy in the information supplied by the manufacturer.|Art. 2(59)
Distinguish "post-market surveillance" from "market surveillance".|Post-market surveillance: all activities carried out by manufacturers, with other economic operators, to set up and keep up to date a systematic procedure to proactively collect and review experience from devices they place on the market, to identify any need for corrective or preventive action. Market surveillance: activities and measures of competent authorities to check and ensure that devices comply with Union law and do not endanger health, safety or other public interests.|Art. 2(60)–(61)
Distinguish "recall" from "withdrawal".|Recall: any measure aimed at achieving the return of a device that has already been made available to the end user. Withdrawal: any measure aimed at preventing a device in the supply chain from being further made available on the market.|Art. 2(62)–(63)
Define "incident".|Any malfunction or deterioration in the characteristics or performance of a device made available on the market, including use error due to ergonomic features, as well as any inadequacy in the information supplied by the manufacturer and any undesirable side-effect.|Art. 2(64)
Define "serious incident".|Any incident that directly or indirectly led, might have led or might lead to: (a) the death of a patient, user or other person; (b) the temporary or permanent serious deterioration of a patient's, user's or other person's state of health; or (c) a serious public health threat.|Art. 2(65)
Define "serious public health threat".|An event which could result in imminent risk of death, serious deterioration in a person's state of health, or serious illness, that may require prompt remedial action, and that may cause significant morbidity or mortality in humans, or that is unusual or unexpected for the given place and time.|Art. 2(66)
Define "field safety corrective action" (FSCA) and "field safety notice" (FSN).|FSCA: corrective action taken by a manufacturer for technical or medical reasons to prevent or reduce the risk of a serious incident in relation to a device made available on the market. FSN: a communication sent by a manufacturer to users or customers in relation to an FSCA.|Art. 2(68)–(69)
Define "common specifications" (CS).|A set of technical and/or clinical requirements, other than a standard, that provides a means of complying with the legal obligations applicable to a device, process or system.|Art. 2(71)
Name five product types excluded from the MDR's scope by Art. 1(6).|IVDs (Regulation 2017/746); medicinal products (Directive 2001/83/EC); advanced therapy medicinal products (Regulation 1394/2007); human blood, blood products, plasma or blood cells; cosmetic products (Regulation 1223/2009); viable transplants, tissues or cells of human or animal origin and their derivatives (non-viable or devitalised tissue derivatives are covered); other products containing viable biological material or organisms; food (Regulation 178/2002).|Art. 1(6)
Which products without an intended medical purpose fall under the MDR?|The groups of products listed in Annex XVI (e.g. coloured contact lenses, dermal fillers, liposuction equipment, lasers/IPL for skin treatment, brain stimulation equipment). They are covered once common specifications apply (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2022/2346, applicable from 22 June 2023).|Art. 1(2)
How is a device that incorporates a medicinal substance with an ancillary action regulated?|It is a device under the MDR (class III under Rule 14). The notified body must obtain a scientific opinion from a medicines authority (a national competent authority or the EMA) on the quality, safety and usefulness of the substance. If the substance's action is principal rather than ancillary, the product is a medicinal product under Directive 2001/83/EC.|Art. 1(8)
How does the MDR treat an integral drug-device combination regulated as a medicinal product (e.g. a prefilled syringe)?|The product is governed by the medicines legislation, but the device part must meet the relevant general safety and performance requirements of Annex I MDR. The marketing authorisation dossier must include the device's declaration of conformity or certificate, or a notified body opinion on the device part (Art. 117).|Art. 1(9), 117
Who decides on the regulatory status of a borderline product?|The Commission, on its own initiative or at the duly substantiated request of a Member State, after consulting the MDCG, by means of implementing acts.|Art. 4
