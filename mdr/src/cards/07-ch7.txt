#VII|Chapter VII|Post-market surveillance, vigilance and market surveillance|Art. 83–100
What is the manufacturer's post-market surveillance system (Art. 83)?|For each device, a PMS system planned, established, documented, implemented, maintained and updated in proportion to the risk class and type of device, as an integral part of the QMS. It actively and systematically gathers, records and analyses data on quality, performance and safety throughout the device's lifetime, to draw conclusions and to determine, implement and monitor preventive and corrective actions.|Art. 83(1)–(2)
Name five uses of PMS data under Art. 83(3).|Update the benefit-risk determination and risk management; update design and manufacturing information, instructions for use and labelling; update the clinical evaluation; update the SSCP; identify needs for preventive, corrective or field safety corrective action; identify options to improve usability, performance and safety; contribute to PMS of other devices; detect and report trends (Art. 88).|Art. 83(3)
What is the PMS plan and where is it described?|The plan on which the PMS system is based (Art. 84). Its requirements are in Annex III section 1.1, and for devices other than custom-made it is part of the technical documentation.|Art. 84
Which classes need a PMS report and which a PSUR?|Class I: a PMS report (Art. 85), updated when necessary and made available to the competent authority on request. Class IIa, IIb and III: a periodic safety update report (PSUR, Art. 86).|Art. 85–86
How often must the PSUR be updated?|Class IIb and class III: at least annually. Class IIa: when necessary and at least every two years.|Art. 86(1)
What must a PSUR contain?|A summary of the results and conclusions of PMS data analysis with the rationale and description of preventive and corrective actions, plus: conclusions of the benefit-risk determination; main PMCF findings; and sales volume with an estimate of the size and characteristics of the population using the device and, where practicable, its usage frequency.|Art. 86(1)
How is the PSUR handled for class III and implantable devices?|The manufacturer submits it via Eudamed to the notified body involved in conformity assessment; the notified body reviews it and adds its evaluation, with details of any action taken, to Eudamed. For other devices, the PSUR is made available to the notified body and, on request, competent authorities.|Art. 86(2)–(3)
What must manufacturers report under Art. 87(1)?|(a) Any serious incident involving devices made available on the Union market, except expected side-effects clearly documented in the product information, quantified in the technical documentation and subject to trend reporting; (b) any FSCA for devices made available on the Union market, including FSCAs in third countries for devices also legally on the Union market, unless the reason is limited to the third-country device.|Art. 87(1)
What are the reporting deadlines for serious incidents?|Immediately after establishing a causal relationship (or that one is reasonably possible), and at most 15 days after becoming aware. Serious public health threat: at most 2 days. Death or unanticipated serious deterioration in health: at most 10 days. An initial incomplete report may be followed by a complete one.|Art. 87(3)–(5)
When must an FSCA be reported?|In advance of the FSCA being undertaken, except in urgent cases where the manufacturer must act immediately.|Art. 87(8)
What are periodic summary reports?|For similar serious incidents with the same device or device type whose root cause is identified or for which an FSCA is implemented, or that are common and well documented, the manufacturer may submit periodic summary reports instead of individual reports, if the coordinating competent authority agrees the format, content and frequency.|Art. 87(9)
What must be reported under trend reporting (Art. 88)?|Any statistically significant increase in the frequency or severity of incidents that are not serious incidents, or of expected undesirable side-effects, that could significantly affect the benefit-risk analysis and has led or may lead to unacceptable risks. The comparison is to the foreseeable frequency or severity in the technical documentation; the method and observation period are set in the PMS plan.|Art. 88
What must the manufacturer do after reporting a serious incident?|Investigate without delay, including a risk assessment of the incident and any FSCA; cooperate with competent authorities and the notified body; and not alter the device or a sample of the batch in a way that could affect evaluation of the causes before informing the competent authorities.|Art. 89(1)
What must a field safety notice contain and how is it handled?|It is sent without delay to users in the required languages. Except in urgent cases, the draft goes to the competent authority for comments. It must identify the devices (incl. UDIs) and the manufacturer (incl. SRN), explain clearly, without understating the risk, the reasons for the FSCA, and state all actions users must take. It is published in Eudamed.|Art. 89(8)
What is the coordinating competent authority in vigilance?|For serious incidents or FSCAs affecting more than one Member State, a coordinating competent authority leads the assessment, by default that of the Member State where the manufacturer (or its authorised representative) has its registered place of business.|Art. 89(9)
How are healthcare professionals and patients involved in vigilance?|Member States encourage and enable healthcare professionals, users and patients to report suspected serious incidents to competent authorities (e.g. via information campaigns and standard web forms). Authorities record these reports centrally and inform the manufacturer.|Art. 87(10)–(11)
What does the Eudamed vigilance and PMS system collect?|Manufacturers' serious incident and FSCA reports, periodic summary reports, trend reports, PSURs, field safety notices, and information exchanged between competent authorities and with the Commission.|Art. 92(1)
Name five market surveillance powers or duties of competent authorities.|Check conformity and performance, incl. document reviews and physical or laboratory checks on samples; draw up annual surveillance activity plans with adequate resources; require documentation from economic operators; enter premises and carry out announced and, if necessary, unannounced inspections; obtain samples free of charge; destroy or render inoperable devices presenting an unacceptable risk or falsified devices; review their surveillance activities at least every four years.|Art. 93
What happens when a device presents an unacceptable risk (Art. 95)?|The competent authority requires the economic operators to take appropriate corrective action without delay: bring the device into compliance, restrict its making available, impose specific requirements, withdraw or recall it within a defined period. It notifies the Commission, other Member States and the notified body via Eudamed; if operators fail to act, it takes provisional measures, which may trigger the Union procedure.|Art. 95
How are non-compliances without unacceptable risk handled (Art. 97)?|E.g. incorrect CE marking, missing declaration of conformity, missing registration or technical documentation: the competent authority requires the economic operator to end the non-compliance within a reasonable, proportionate period. If it persists, the Member State restricts or prohibits making the device available or ensures recall or withdrawal.|Art. 97
What are preventive health protection measures (Art. 98)?|Where a Member State's evaluation shows a potential risk related to a device or a category or group of devices, it may take justified transitional measures to prohibit, restrict or set requirements for their making available, or to withdraw or recall them. The Commission may then adopt Union-wide implementing measures.|Art. 98
Which principles of good administrative practice apply to authority measures?|Each measure states its exact grounds, is notified without delay to the operator with information on remedies, and, unless urgent, the operator may first make submissions. Measures are withdrawn or amended promptly once the operator shows effective action.|Art. 99
What does the Eudamed market surveillance system collect?|Summaries of market surveillance results, final inspection reports, information on devices presenting unacceptable risks, on non-compliance, on preventive health protection measures, and summaries of reviews of market surveillance activities.|Art. 100
