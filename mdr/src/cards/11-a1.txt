#AI|Annex I|General safety and performance requirements (GSPR)|Annex I
How is Annex I structured?|Chapter I: general requirements (sections 1–9); Chapter II: requirements regarding design and manufacture (sections 10–22); Chapter III: requirements regarding the information supplied with the device, i.e. label and instructions for use (section 23).|Annex I
What does GSPR 1 require?|Devices achieve the performance intended by the manufacturer and are designed and manufactured so that, in normal conditions of use, they are suitable for their intended purpose. They are safe and effective and do not compromise the clinical condition or safety of patients or the safety and health of users or others, provided risks are acceptable when weighed against the benefits to the patient and compatible with a high level of protection, taking into account the generally acknowledged state of the art.|Annex I 1
What does "reduce risks as far as possible" mean in GSPR 2?|Reducing risks as far as possible without adversely affecting the benefit-risk ratio.|Annex I 2
List the risk management steps in GSPR 3.|(a) Establish and document a risk management plan for each device; (b) identify and analyse known and foreseeable hazards; (c) estimate and evaluate risks during intended use and reasonably foreseeable misuse; (d) eliminate or control those risks per GSPR 4; (e) evaluate the impact of production and PMS information on hazards, risks, overall risk, benefit-risk ratio and acceptability; (f) amend control measures if necessary. It is a continuous iterative process over the whole life cycle.|Annex I 3
In which order of priority must risk control measures be applied (GSPR 4)?|(a) Eliminate or reduce risks as far as possible through safe design and manufacture; (b) where appropriate, take adequate protection measures, incl. alarms if necessary, for risks that cannot be eliminated; (c) provide information for safety (warnings, precautions, contra-indications) and, where appropriate, training to users. Users must be informed of any residual risks.|Annex I 4
What does GSPR 5 require regarding use error?|Reduce as far as possible the risks related to the ergonomic features of the device and its use environment (design for patient safety), and take into account the technical knowledge, experience, education, training and use environment, and the medical and physical conditions of intended users (design for lay, professional, disabled or other users).|Annex I 5
What do GSPR 6 and 7 require?|GSPR 6: characteristics and performance must not be adversely affected, to the point of compromising health or safety, during the lifetime indicated by the manufacturer under the stresses of normal use and proper maintenance. GSPR 7: design, manufacture and packaging must ensure transport and storage (e.g. temperature and humidity fluctuations) do not adversely affect characteristics and performance.|Annex I 6–7
What does GSPR 8 require?|All known and foreseeable risks and any undesirable side-effects are minimised and acceptable when weighed against the evaluated benefits to the patient and/or user arising from the device's achieved performance during normal conditions of use.|Annex I 8
How does GSPR 9 apply the safety requirements to Annex XVI products?|For products without an intended medical purpose, sections 1 and 8 mean the device, used as intended, presents no risk at all, or no more than the maximum acceptable risk related to the product's use consistent with a high level of protection of health and safety.|Annex I 9
What limit applies to CMR and endocrine-disrupting substances in certain devices (GSPR 10.4.1)?|Invasive devices in direct contact with the body, or devices that (re)administer or transport or store medicines, body liquids or other substances, may contain CMR substances of category 1A or 1B or endocrine-disrupting substances in a concentration above 0.1% weight by weight only if justified per section 10.4.2.|Annex I 10.4.1
What must the justification for CMR or endocrine-disrupting substances above 0.1% w/w cover?|An analysis and estimation of patient or user exposure; an analysis of possible alternative substances, materials or designs and their availability (incl. independent research, peer-reviewed studies and scientific committee opinions); why substitutes or design changes are inappropriate for functionality, performance and benefit-risk, incl. for children and pregnant or breastfeeding women; and the latest relevant scientific committee guidelines (SCHEER).|Annex I 10.4.2
What labelling applies when CMR or endocrine-disrupting substances exceed 0.1% w/w?|Their presence is labelled on the device and/or the packaging of each unit or the sales packaging, with the list of substances. If use includes children, pregnant or breastfeeding women or other vulnerable groups, a specific justification is in the technical documentation and the IFU gives information on residual risks and precautionary measures.|Annex I 10.4.5
What do the GSPR require for nanomaterials?|Devices are designed and manufactured to reduce as far as possible the risks related to the size and properties of particles, incl. nanomaterials, released or releasable into the patient's or user's body, unless they contact only intact skin. Special care applies where internal exposure potential is high or medium (see Rule 19).|Annex I 10.6
What do the GSPR require for software (section 17)?|Repeatability, reliability and performance in line with intended use, with means to reduce risks in a single fault condition (17.1); development per the state of the art, considering the development life cycle, risk management incl. information security, verification and validation (17.2); account of mobile platform features and external factors (17.3); manufacturers set minimum requirements for hardware, IT network characteristics and IT security measures, incl. protection against unauthorised access (17.4).|Annex I 17
What do the GSPR require for devices used by lay persons (section 22)?|Appropriate performance considering lay persons' skills and means and the variation in their technique and environment; information and instructions easy to understand and apply; reduced risk of error in handling, preparation and interpretation; and, where reasonably possible, a way for the lay person to verify that the device works as intended and to be warned if it failed to give a valid result.|Annex I 22
Name eight items that must appear on the label (GSPR 23.2).|Device name or trade name; details to identify the device, its content and intended purpose; manufacturer name and address; authorised representative name and address (if outside the EU); medicinal substance, blood/plasma derivative or tissue content; CMR/ED labelling; lot or serial number; UDI carrier; use-by date (at least year and month) or date of manufacture; storage and handling conditions; sterile state and method; warnings and precautions; single use; reprocessed single-use status and cycles; "custom-made device"; indication that it is a medical device; composition for absorbed substances; "exclusively for clinical investigation".|Annex I 23.2
When may instructions for use be omitted or provided electronically?|IFU may be omitted for class I and IIa devices that can be used safely without them. IFU may be provided electronically instead of on paper only as allowed by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/2226 on electronic instructions for use, which is limited to devices for professional users.|Annex I 23.1(d), (f)
