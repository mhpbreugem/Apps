#AXIV|Annex XIV–XV|Clinical evaluation, PMCF and clinical investigations|Annex XIV–XV
What is post-market clinical follow-up (PMCF)?|A continuous process that updates the clinical evaluation and is part of the PMS plan. The manufacturer proactively collects and evaluates clinical data from the use of the CE-marked device within its intended purpose, to confirm safety and performance throughout the expected lifetime, ensure the continued acceptability of identified risks, and detect emerging risks on the basis of factual evidence.|Annex XIV Part B 5
Name five elements of a PMCF plan.|General PMCF methods (gathering clinical experience, user feedback, screening literature and other clinical data sources); specific methods (registers, PMCF studies); rationale for their appropriateness; references to the clinical evaluation report and risk management; specific objectives; evaluation of data on equivalent or similar devices; references to CS, harmonised standards and guidance; a detailed, justified time schedule.|Annex XIV Part B 6
What happens with PMCF results?|They are documented in a PMCF evaluation report, part of the clinical evaluation report and technical documentation, and taken into account in the clinical evaluation and risk management. If preventive or corrective measures are needed, the manufacturer implements them.|Annex XIV Part B 7–8
How is Annex XV structured?|Chapter I: general requirements (ethical principles, methods); Chapter II: documentation for the application (application form, investigator's brochure, clinical investigation plan, other information such as the GSPR statement); Chapter III: other obligations of the sponsor.|Annex XV
Which ethical principles govern clinical investigations?|Every step, from considering the need and justification for the study to publishing the results, follows recognised ethical principles, such as the World Medical Association's Declaration of Helsinki.|Annex XV Ch. I 1
How long must the sponsor keep clinical investigation documentation?|At least 10 years after the investigation ends or, if the device is then placed on the market, at least 10 years after the last device is placed on the market; 15 years for implantable devices.|Annex XV Ch. III 3
