#VI|Chapter VI|Clinical evaluation and clinical investigations|Art. 61–82
On what must confirmation of conformity with the GSPR be based (Art. 61(1))?|On clinical data providing sufficient clinical evidence, including relevant PMS data (Annex III). The manufacturer specifies and justifies the level of clinical evidence needed, appropriate to the characteristics and intended purpose of the device, and plans, conducts and documents a clinical evaluation per Art. 61 and Annex XIV Part A.|Art. 61(1), (3)
Before its clinical evaluation, whom may the manufacturer of a class III or class IIb drug-delivery device consult?|An expert panel, to review its intended clinical development strategy and proposals for clinical investigation. The manufacturer must give due consideration to the panel's views.|Art. 61(2)
For which devices are clinical investigations required by default?|Implantable devices and class III devices, unless an exemption in Art. 61(4)–(6) applies.|Art. 61(4)
When can a class III or implantable device be exempt from a new clinical investigation based on equivalence?|When it modifies a device already marketed by the same manufacturer, equivalence is demonstrated and endorsed by the notified body and the clinical evaluation suffices; or when it is equivalent to another manufacturer's marketed device and a contract explicitly allows full, ongoing access to that technical documentation, with the original clinical evaluation done per the MDR.|Art. 61(4)–(5)
Which other cases are exempt from mandatory clinical investigations under Art. 61(6)?|(a) Devices lawfully placed on the market under the former directives whose clinical evaluation is based on sufficient clinical data and complies with product-specific CS where available; (b) sutures, staples, dental fillings, dental braces, tooth crowns, screws, wedges, plates, wires, pins, clips and connectors, with sufficient clinical data and compliance with CS where available.|Art. 61(6)
When may conformity be shown without clinical data (Art. 61(10))?|Where clinical data are not deemed appropriate, with adequate justification based on the risk management results and the device-body interaction, the intended clinical performance and the manufacturer's claims. The adequacy of non-clinical methods alone (performance evaluation, bench testing, pre-clinical evaluation) must be substantiated in the technical documentation.|Art. 61(10)
What are the three kinds of characteristics compared to demonstrate equivalence?|Technical (design, conditions of use, specifications and properties, deployment methods, principles of operation, critical performance requirements, software algorithms); biological (same materials or substances in contact with the same tissues or body fluids for similar duration and release characteristics); clinical (same clinical condition or purpose, same site in the body, similar population, same kind of user, similar critical performance). There must be no clinically significant difference in safety and clinical performance.|Annex XIV Part A 3
How is the clinical evaluation documented and kept current?|In a clinical evaluation report (CER), part of the technical documentation. The evaluation is updated throughout the device's life cycle with data from the PMCF plan and the PMS plan; for class III and implantable devices, the PMCF evaluation report (and SSCP if indicated) is updated at least annually.|Art. 61(11)–(12)
List the five stages of the clinical evaluation process in Annex XIV Part A.|(1) Establish and update a clinical evaluation plan; (2) identify available clinical data and gaps through a systematic scientific literature review; (3) appraise all relevant clinical data for suitability; (4) generate any new or additional clinical data needed through properly designed clinical investigations; (5) analyse all relevant data to reach conclusions on safety and clinical performance, including clinical benefits.|Annex XIV Part A 1
Name five items a clinical evaluation plan must contain.|The GSPR requiring clinical data; the intended purpose; target groups with indications and contra-indications; intended clinical benefits with clinical outcome parameters; methods to examine clinical safety (residual risks, side-effects); parameters for benefit-risk acceptability based on the state of the art; how benefit-risk of specific components is addressed; a clinical development plan from exploratory to confirmatory investigations and PMCF, with milestones and acceptance criteria.|Annex XIV Part A 1(a)
For which three purposes are clinical investigations conducted under Art. 62(1)?|(a) To establish and verify that the device achieves its intended performance under normal conditions of use; (b) to establish and verify the clinical benefits specified by the manufacturer; (c) to establish and verify clinical safety, determine undesirable side-effects, and assess whether they are acceptable risks weighed against the benefits.|Art. 62(1)
What must a sponsor established outside the EU do?|Ensure that a natural or legal person is established in the Union as its legal representative, responsible for ensuring the sponsor's obligations and acting as addressee of all communications. Member States may waive this for investigations only on their territory, if a contact person is established there.|Art. 62(2)
What ethical review requirements apply to clinical investigations?|They are subject to scientific and ethical review; the ethical review is performed by an ethics committee under national law, and at least one lay person takes part. The investigation may not proceed if the ethics committee has issued a negative opinion valid for the whole Member State.|Art. 62(3)–(4)
Name five conditions for conducting a clinical investigation under Art. 62(4).|Authorisation by the Member State(s); no negative ethics committee opinion; sponsor or legal representative in the Union; protection of vulnerable subjects; anticipated benefits justify foreseeable risks (monitored constantly); informed consent; rights to integrity, privacy and data protection safeguarded; as little pain, discomfort and fear as possible; medical care under a qualified doctor (or dentist); no undue influence, incl. financial; device conforms to the GSPR apart from aspects under investigation.|Art. 62(4)
What must informed consent include?|Written, dated and signed by the interviewer and the subject (or legally designated representative) after being informed of: the nature, objectives, benefits, implications, risks and inconveniences; the subject's rights, incl. to refuse and withdraw at any time without detriment or justification; the conditions and expected duration; possible treatment alternatives; the compensation system; the EU-wide unique identification number of the investigation; and availability of results. The subject receives a copy.|Art. 63
Which subject groups receive special protection in clinical investigations?|Incapacitated subjects (Art. 64), minors (Art. 65), pregnant or breastfeeding women (Art. 66); Member States may add measures for persons in military service, deprived of liberty, excluded by judicial decision, or in residential care (Art. 67); and there are special rules for emergency situations (Art. 68).|Art. 64–68
How is damage to subjects compensated?|Member States ensure systems for compensation of damage suffered by a subject from participation in a clinical investigation on their territory, in the form of insurance, a guarantee or an equivalent arrangement appropriate to the nature and extent of the risk.|Art. 69
What are the validation timelines for a clinical investigation application?|The sponsor applies via Eudamed with the Annex XV Chapter II documentation. Within 10 days the Member State notifies whether the investigation is in scope and the application complete. If not, the sponsor has up to 10 days to complete it (extendable by up to 20 days).|Art. 70(1)–(3)
When may a clinical investigation start after validation?|For class I or non-invasive class IIa/IIb investigational devices: immediately after the validation date, unless national law provides otherwise and provided no negative ethics committee opinion was issued. For other devices: once the Member State has authorised it, which it must decide within 45 days of the validation date (extendable by 20 days for expert consultation).|Art. 70(7)
What is required for a PMCF investigation with a CE-marked device used within its intended purpose?|If subjects undergo additional procedures that are invasive or burdensome, the sponsor notifies the Member States concerned via Eudamed at least 30 days before it starts. Using a CE-marked device outside its intended purpose triggers the full Art. 62–81 regime.|Art. 74
What is the procedure for a substantial modification to a clinical investigation?|The sponsor notifies the Member States via Eudamed within one week, with updated documentation. It may implement the change at the earliest 38 days after notification, unless the Member State refuses or an ethics committee issues a negative opinion; the Member State may extend the period by 7 days to consult experts.|Art. 75
What must the sponsor report at the end, temporary halt or early termination of an investigation?|Inform the Member States within 15 days of a temporary halt or early termination (within 24 hours if for safety reasons) and within 15 days of the end. Submit the clinical investigation report, with a summary understandable to the intended user, within one year of the end, or within three months of early termination or a temporary halt, regardless of the outcome.|Art. 77
When does the coordinated assessment procedure for multinational investigations become mandatory?|It is voluntary for Member States until 26 May 2027 and applies to all Member States from 27 May 2027. A coordinating Member State leads the assessment of a single application.|Art. 78(14)
Which events must the sponsor record, and which must it report without delay?|Record: adverse events identified as critical in the CIP, serious adverse events, device deficiencies that might have led to an SAE, and new findings. Report without delay via Eudamed: SAEs with a causal relationship to the device, comparator or procedure (or where one is reasonably possible), device deficiencies that might have led to an SAE, and new findings on these.|Art. 80(1)–(2)
What rules apply to clinical investigations not done for conformity assessment purposes?|Art. 82: they must meet core requirements of Art. 62 (e.g. ethics, protection of subjects, consent, qualified investigators) and any additional national rules Member States adopt.|Art. 82
What are the requirements for investigators and investigation sites?|The investigator must practise a profession recognised in the Member State as qualifying for that role, with the necessary scientific knowledge and patient-care experience; other staff must be suitably qualified. Facilities must be suitable and similar to those where the device is intended to be used.|Art. 62(6)–(7)
