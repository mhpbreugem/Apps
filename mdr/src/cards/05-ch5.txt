#V|Chapter V|Classification and conformity assessment|Art. 51–60
What are the MDR risk classes and what determines the class?|Class I, IIa, IIb and III, based on the intended purpose of the device and its inherent risks, using the classification rules of Annex VIII.|Art. 51(1)
Who resolves a classification dispute between a manufacturer and its notified body?|The competent authority of the Member State where the manufacturer has its registered place of business (or its authorised representative, if the manufacturer is outside the EU). If the notified body is in another Member State, that authority first consults the authority that designated the notified body.|Art. 51(2)
Which conformity assessment routes are available for class III devices?|Annex IX (quality management system plus assessment of the technical documentation), or Annex X (type-examination) combined with Annex XI (production quality assurance, Part A, or product verification, Part B).|Art. 52(3)
Which conformity assessment routes are available for class IIb devices?|Annex IX Chapters I and III, with technical documentation assessment of at least one representative device per generic device group (for class IIb implantables, every device, except WET implants such as sutures, staples, screws, plates, wires, pins, clips and connectors); or Annex X type-examination combined with Annex XI.|Art. 52(4)
Which conformity assessment routes are available for class IIa devices?|Annex IX Chapters I and III, with technical documentation assessment of at least one representative device per category of devices; or technical documentation per Annexes II and III combined with Annex XI (Part A production quality assurance or Part B product verification).|Art. 52(6)
How do class I devices reach conformity, and when is a notified body still involved?|The manufacturer draws up the technical documentation (Annexes II and III) and issues the EU declaration of conformity (self-declaration). A notified body is needed only for sterile devices (Is), devices with a measuring function (Im) and reusable surgical instruments (Ir), limited to sterility, metrology or reuse aspects respectively, via Annex IX Chapters I and III or Annex XI Part A.|Art. 52(7)
Which procedure applies to custom-made devices?|The manufacturer follows Annex XIII and draws up the statement in its section 1 before placing the device on the market. Class III implantable custom-made devices also need the Annex IX Chapter I QMS procedure or Annex XI Part A.|Art. 52(8)
Which devices go through the clinical evaluation consultation procedure (CECP)?|Class III implantable devices and class IIb active devices intended to administer and/or remove a medicinal product (Rule 12). The notified body's clinical evaluation assessment report goes via the Commission to an expert panel, which decides whether to give a scientific opinion.|Art. 54(1)
What are the three exemptions from the CECP?|(a) Renewal of a certificate issued under the MDR; (b) a modification of a device already marketed by the same manufacturer for the same intended purpose, where the manufacturer shows the changes do not adversely affect the benefit-risk ratio; (c) the clinical evaluation principles for that device type are set out in common specifications and the notified body confirms the clinical evaluation complies with them.|Art. 54(2)
What are the expert panel's deadlines in the CECP?|The expert panel decides within 21 days whether to provide a scientific opinion, and if so, delivers it within 60 days of receiving the documentation. The notified body must give due consideration to the opinion and justify any divergence.|Art. 54; Annex IX 5.1
What is the scrutiny mechanism of Art. 55?|Notified bodies notify the competent authorities, via Eudamed, of certificates granted after a CECP, with the SSCP, the notified body's assessment report, the instructions for use and the expert panel's opinion (and justification where views diverged). Authorities may then take further action.|Art. 55
How long are notified body certificates valid?|For the period indicated, at most five years. They may be extended for further periods of at most five years each, based on a re-assessment. Their content follows Annex XII.|Art. 56(2)
What must an agreement on a voluntary change of notified body cover?|An agreement between the manufacturer, the incoming notified body and (where practicable) the outgoing one, covering: the date the outgoing body's certificates become invalid; until when its number may be shown in information supplied, incl. promotional material; transfer of documents; the date the incoming body takes over; and the last serial or lot number for which the outgoing body is responsible.|Art. 58
When may a competent authority allow a device on the market without the required conformity assessment?|Under Art. 59, on a duly justified request, a competent authority may authorise a specific device in its territory if its use is in the interest of public health or patient safety or health. The Commission may extend such an authorisation to the whole Union in exceptional cases.|Art. 59
What is a certificate of free sale?|For export, at the manufacturer's or AR's request, the Member State of its registered place of business issues a certificate declaring that it is properly registered and that the CE-marked device may be legally placed on the Union market. Valid for at most five years and never beyond the device's certificate.|Art. 60
