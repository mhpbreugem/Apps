#II|Chapter II|Making available, economic operators, CE marking|Art. 5–24
What is the basic condition for placing a device on the market (Art. 5)?|A device may be placed on the market or put into service only if it complies with the MDR when duly supplied and properly installed, maintained and used in accordance with its intended purpose. It must meet the applicable general safety and performance requirements of Annex I, and demonstration of conformity includes a clinical evaluation (Art. 61).|Art. 5(1)–(3)
Name four conditions under which health institutions may manufacture and use devices in-house without full MDR compliance.|The devices meet Annex I GSPR and: are not transferred to another legal entity; are made and used under an appropriate QMS; the institution justifies that the target patient group's needs cannot be met (or not at the right level of performance) by an equivalent device on the market; information is provided to the competent authority on request; a public declaration is drawn up; documentation of the facility, process, design and performance is kept; clinical experience is reviewed with corrective action. Not allowed for industrial-scale manufacture.|Art. 5(5)
Does the MDR apply to devices sold online or to remote diagnostic services?|Yes. A device offered via information society services (e.g. online) to a person in the Union must comply with the MDR. So must a device not placed on the market but used in a commercial activity to provide a diagnostic or therapeutic service to a person in the Union. On request, the provider must give the competent authority a copy of the EU declaration of conformity.|Art. 6
Which misleading claims does Art. 7 prohibit?|In labelling, instructions for use, making available, putting into service and advertising, it is prohibited to mislead the user or patient about intended purpose, safety and performance by: ascribing functions or properties the device does not have; creating a false impression about treatment, diagnosis, functions or properties; failing to inform of a likely risk; or suggesting uses other than the intended purpose for which conformity was assessed.|Art. 7
What is the legal effect of applying harmonised standards?|Devices in conformity with harmonised standards (or parts of them) whose references are published in the Official Journal are presumed to conform to the MDR requirements covered by those standards. This also applies to system or process requirements, such as the QMS, risk management, PMS, clinical investigations, clinical evaluation and PMCF.|Art. 8
When may the Commission adopt common specifications, and how binding are they?|Where no harmonised standards exist, they are insufficient, or public health concerns must be addressed, the Commission may adopt CS by implementing acts after consulting the MDCG. Devices complying with CS are presumed to conform. Manufacturers must comply with CS unless they can duly justify solutions that give at least an equivalent level of safety and performance.|Art. 9
Name five general obligations of manufacturers under Art. 10.|Design and manufacture per the MDR; a risk management system; clinical evaluation including PMCF; technical documentation (Annexes II and III); conformity assessment, EU declaration of conformity and CE marking; UDI and registration obligations; keeping documentation available; a QMS; a PMS system; information supplied with the device in the required languages; corrective action; reporting serious incidents and FSCAs; cooperation with competent authorities; financial coverage for liability; a person responsible for regulatory compliance.|Art. 10
How long must manufacturers keep the technical documentation, EU declaration of conformity and certificates?|At least 10 years after the last device covered by the EU declaration of conformity has been placed on the market; at least 15 years for implantable devices.|Art. 10(8)
Name six aspects the manufacturer's QMS must address at least (Art. 10(9)).|(a) regulatory compliance strategy, incl. conformity assessment and management of modifications; (b) identification of applicable GSPR; (c) management responsibility; (d) resource management, incl. selection and control of suppliers and subcontractors; (e) risk management; (f) clinical evaluation incl. PMCF; (g) product realisation; (h) verification of UDI assignments; (i) the PMS system; (j) communication with authorities, notified bodies, operators and customers; (k) vigilance reporting of serious incidents and FSCAs; (l) CAPA and verification of effectiveness; (m) monitoring, measurement, data analysis and product improvement.|Art. 10(9)
What does Art. 10(16) require regarding liability?|People may claim compensation for damage caused by a defective device under Union and national law. Manufacturers must have measures in place, proportionate to the risk class, type of device and enterprise size, to provide sufficient financial coverage for their potential liability under the Product Liability Directive 85/374/EEC.|Art. 10(16)
What does Art. 10a require, and which regulation introduced it?|Introduced by Regulation (EU) 2024/1860 (applicable from 10 January 2025). A manufacturer that anticipates an interruption or discontinuation of supply of a device (other than custom-made), where this could foreseeably cause serious harm or a risk of serious harm to patients or public health, must inform the competent authority, and the economic operators, health institutions and healthcare professionals it directly supplies, at least 6 months in advance (except in exceptional circumstances).|Art. 10a
When must a manufacturer appoint an authorised representative, and how many?|When the manufacturer is not established in a Member State. The device may only be placed on the Union market if the manufacturer designates a sole authorised representative.|Art. 11(1)
Name four tasks the authorised representative's mandate must include at least.|Verify that the EU declaration of conformity and technical documentation exist and that conformity assessment was done; keep copies available for authorities; comply with registration obligations and verify the manufacturer's; provide information and documentation to authorities on request; forward requests for samples or access; cooperate on preventive and corrective actions; immediately inform the manufacturer of complaints and reports; terminate the mandate if the manufacturer acts contrary to its obligations.|Art. 11(3)
What is the liability of the authorised representative?|Where the manufacturer is not established in a Member State and has not complied with its obligations under Art. 10, the authorised representative is legally liable for defective devices on the same basis as, and jointly and severally with, the manufacturer.|Art. 11(5)
What must an agreement on a change of authorised representative cover?|An agreement between the manufacturer, the incoming AR and (where practicable) the outgoing AR, covering at least: the end date of the old mandate and start date of the new one; until when the outgoing AR may be indicated in information supplied, incl. promotional material; transfer of documents, incl. confidentiality and property rights; the outgoing AR's duty after the mandate ends to forward complaints and reports.|Art. 12
Name five checks an importer must make before placing a device on the market.|CE marking and EU declaration of conformity present; manufacturer identified and an authorised representative designated; labelled per the MDR and accompanied by instructions for use; UDI assigned where applicable; registered in Eudamed. Importers must also add their own name and address on the device, packaging or accompanying document.|Art. 13(2)–(3)
Name four ongoing obligations of importers besides the pre-market checks.|Ensure storage and transport conditions do not affect compliance; keep a register of complaints, non-conforming devices, recalls and withdrawals; inform the manufacturer and AR of non-conformity (and the competent authority of serious risk or falsification); forward complaints; cooperate with authorities; keep a copy of the EU declaration of conformity and certificates for the retention period.|Art. 13
What must a distributor verify before making a device available?|That the device has the CE marking and an EU declaration of conformity; is accompanied by the information supplied by the manufacturer; for imported devices, that the importer has complied with its labelling duties (Art. 13(3)); and that a UDI has been assigned where applicable. A representative sampling method may be used.|Art. 14(2)
What must a distributor do if it considers a device non-compliant?|Not make it available until it complies; inform the manufacturer and, where applicable, the AR and importer; and, where the device presents a serious risk or is falsified, also inform the competent authority of its Member State.|Art. 14(2)
What qualifications must the person responsible for regulatory compliance (PRRC) have?|Either (a) a diploma or equivalent qualification in law, medicine, pharmacy, engineering or another relevant scientific discipline plus at least one year of professional experience in regulatory affairs or QMS for medical devices; or (b) four years of professional experience in regulatory affairs or QMS for medical devices. For custom-made device manufacturers: at least two years of experience in a relevant field of manufacturing.|Art. 15(1)
What is the PRRC responsible for?|Ensuring at least that: conformity of the devices is checked per the QMS before release; the technical documentation and EU declaration of conformity are drawn up and kept up to date; PMS obligations are met; vigilance reporting obligations (Art. 87–91) are fulfilled; and, for investigational devices, the statement in Annex XV Chapter II section 4.1 is issued.|Art. 15(3)
Must a micro or small enterprise employ a PRRC?|No. Micro and small enterprises (Recommendation 2003/361/EC) do not need the PRRC within their organisation but must have one permanently and continuously at their disposal. Authorised representatives must also have a PRRC permanently and continuously at their disposal.|Art. 15(2), (6)
How does the MDR protect the PRRC's independence?|The PRRC must suffer no disadvantage within the manufacturer's organisation for properly fulfilling their duties, whether or not they are employees. Where responsibility is shared, each person's area of responsibility must be stipulated in writing.|Art. 15(4)–(5)
In which three cases does a distributor, importer or other person take on the manufacturer's obligations?|When it: (a) makes a device available under its own name, registered trade name or registered trade mark (unless an agreement keeps the manufacturer identified on the label and responsible); (b) changes the intended purpose of a device already placed on the market or put into service; (c) modifies such a device in a way that may affect compliance.|Art. 16(1)
Which two activities do not turn a distributor or importer into a manufacturer?|(a) Providing or translating the information supplied by the manufacturer, and further information needed to market the device in a Member State; (b) changing the outer packaging, incl. pack size, where repackaging is needed for the national market and done so the device's original condition cannot be affected (not if a sterile pack is opened, damaged or negatively affected).|Art. 16(2)
What must a relabeller or repackager do under Art. 16(3)–(4)?|Indicate the activity and its name, registered trade name or trade mark and address on the device or packaging; have a QMS ensuring accurate, up-to-date translation and preservation of the device's original condition; and at least 28 days before making the device available, inform the manufacturer and the competent authority, provide a sample or mock-up on request, and provide a notified body certificate for its QMS.|Art. 16(3)–(4)
Under what conditions may single-use devices be reprocessed?|Only where national law permits it, and only per Art. 17. Whoever reprocesses a single-use device for further use in the Union is deemed its manufacturer and takes on the manufacturer's obligations. Member States may allow health institutions to reprocess in-house under common specifications (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/1207) or equivalent national rules. Only devices placed on the market under the MDR or the former directives may be reprocessed.|Art. 17
What must a manufacturer of an implantable device provide to the patient?|An implant card (with the patient's identity) and information on: device identification (name, serial or lot number, UDI, model, manufacturer name, address and website); warnings, precautions or measures regarding interference with external influences, medical examinations or environmental conditions; expected lifetime and necessary follow-up; and other information for safe use, incl. the materials and substances patients may be exposed to.|Art. 18(1)
Which implants are exempt from the implant card obligation?|Sutures, staples, dental fillings, dental braces, tooth crowns, screws, wedges, plates, wires, pins, clips and connectors.|Art. 18(3)
What is the EU declaration of conformity and what does it state?|A document in which the manufacturer states that the MDR requirements have been fulfilled for the device covered. It contains at least the Annex IV information, is continuously updated and is translated into the languages required by the Member States where the device is made available. One single declaration covers all applicable Union acts. By drawing it up, the manufacturer assumes responsibility for compliance.|Art. 19
Name four rules for affixing the CE marking.|Affixed visibly, legibly and indelibly to the device or its sterile packaging (or, if not possible, the packaging); also in the instructions for use and on sales packaging; affixed before the device is placed on the market; followed by the notified body's identification number where a notified body was involved; may be followed by a pictogram or mark indicating a special risk or use. Not for custom-made or investigational devices.|Art. 20
Which devices may be made available without CE marking under Art. 21?|Investigational devices supplied for clinical investigations meeting Art. 62–80 and 82, and custom-made devices meeting Art. 52(8) and Annex XIII. Non-conforming devices may be shown at trade fairs, exhibitions and demonstrations if a visible sign states they are for presentation only and cannot be made available until they comply.|Art. 21
What must a person who combines CE-marked devices into a system or procedure pack declare?|A statement that: mutual compatibility was verified per the manufacturers' instructions and operations were carried out per those instructions; the system or pack was packaged with relevant user information, incl. the manufacturers' information; and the combining activity is subject to appropriate internal monitoring, verification and validation.|Art. 22(1)–(2)
When is a system or procedure pack treated as a device in its own right?|When it incorporates devices without the CE marking, when the chosen combination is not compatible in view of the devices' original intended purposes, or when sterilisation was not done according to the manufacturer's instructions. It then needs its own conformity assessment under Art. 52.|Art. 22(4)
What does Art. 23 require for spare parts and components?|Whoever makes available an item specifically intended to replace an identical or similar integral part of a defective or worn device, to maintain or re-establish its function without changing its performance, safety characteristics or intended purpose, must ensure the item does not adversely affect safety and performance and keep supporting evidence. An item that significantly changes performance, safety or intended purpose is itself a device.|Art. 23
What does Art. 24 guarantee?|Free movement: Member States shall not refuse, prohibit or restrict the making available or putting into service in their territory of devices that comply with the MDR.|Art. 24
