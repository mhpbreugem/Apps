#X|Chapter X|Final provisions, transition and amendments|Art. 114–123
Which committee assists the Commission under the MDR?|The Committee on Medical Devices, a comitology committee under Regulation (EU) 182/2011. The examination procedure applies to implementing acts; if the committee gives no opinion, the Commission does not adopt the draft.|Art. 114
How do delegated acts under the MDR work?|The Commission's power to adopt delegated acts was conferred for five years from 25 May 2017, tacitly extended; the Parliament or Council can revoke it, and a delegated act enters into force only if neither objects within three months (extendable by three).|Art. 115
Which other EU acts did the MDR amend?|Directive 2001/83/EC on medicinal products (Art. 117, device part of drug-device combinations), Regulation (EC) 178/2002 on food (Art. 118, devices are not food) and Regulation (EC) 1223/2009 on cosmetics (Art. 119, borderline decisions by the Commission).|Art. 117–119
Which MDR requirements apply to legacy devices during the transition?|The MDR requirements on post-market surveillance, market surveillance, vigilance and registration of economic operators and devices apply instead of the corresponding requirements of the directives.|Art. 120(3)
Until when may legacy devices be placed on the market under Regulation (EU) 2023/607?|Until 31 December 2027 for class III devices and class IIb implantable devices (except WET items such as sutures, staples, dental fillings, braces, crowns, screws, wedges, plates, wires, pins, clips and connectors); until 31 December 2028 for other class IIb devices, class IIa devices, and class I devices that are sterile or have a measuring function.|Art. 120(3a)
What conditions must a legacy device meet to use the extended transition?|It continues to comply with the relevant directive; no significant changes in design or intended purpose; no unacceptable risk to health or safety; the manufacturer had an MDR QMS (Art. 10(9)) by 26 May 2024; and the manufacturer lodged a formal application with a notified body by 26 May 2024 and signed a written agreement with it by 26 September 2024.|Art. 120(3c)
Which directive certificates benefit from the 2023/607 extension?|Certificates issued under the directives from 25 May 2017 that were still valid on 26 May 2021 and not withdrawn later. Certificates that expired before 20 March 2023 also qualify if, before expiry, the manufacturer had signed an agreement with a notified body, or a competent authority granted a derogation (Art. 59) or required the conformity assessment (Art. 97).|Art. 120(2)
What happened to the "sell-off" date for legacy devices?|The original Art. 120(4) allowed devices already placed on the market to be made available until 27 May 2025. Regulation (EU) 2023/607 deleted this sell-off date, so devices lawfully placed on the market can remain available without a time limit.|Art. 120(4)
What did Regulation (EU) 2020/561 do?|Adopted in April 2020 in response to COVID-19, it postponed the MDR's date of application by one year, from 26 May 2020 to 26 May 2021, and allowed Union-wide derogations from conformity assessment.|Reg. 2020/561
What did Regulation (EU) 2024/1860 change?|It allowed a gradual, module-by-module roll-out of Eudamed; introduced Art. 10a (advance notice of supply interruption or discontinuation, applicable from 10 January 2025); and extended the IVDR transition periods.|Reg. 2024/1860
When must the Commission evaluate the MDR?|By 27 May 2027, the Commission assesses the application of the MDR and produces an evaluation report on progress towards its objectives.|Art. 121
Which provisions applied before 26 May 2021?|The notified body provisions (Art. 35–50) and Art. 101 and 103 (competent authorities, MDCG) from 26 November 2017; Art. 102 (cooperation) from 26 May 2018.|Art. 123(3)
